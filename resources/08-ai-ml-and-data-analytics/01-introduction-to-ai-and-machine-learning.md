# Introduction to AI and Machine Learning

## Objectives

- Define AI and its subset, machine learning.

## Notes

### Why data comes first

- AI/ML forecasts and automates, while data analytics mines history for insight.
- Shop data (visitors, rush hours, best sellers) could guide bean orders, new sites and drinks, if collected cleanly.

### AI, ML and classical programming

- AI: the broad field of computer systems that do humanlike tasks.
- ML: AI where machines learn complex jobs from data, not spelled-out instructions.
- Classical programming hard-codes a rule from past pairings (a cheese Danish with every caramel latte), ignoring the individual customer and product.

### Training and inference

- Training: ML finds hidden customer-sales patterns in lots of history and outputs a model.
- Inference: the model gets new data, like a fresh order, and predicts or decides.
- Result: suggestions fitted to each customer's order history, and more food sold.

### Conversational and predictive AI

- A counter kiosk could take spoken orders, enter them in the register for baristas and use natural language processing (NLP) to offer an extra shot or the new seasonal latte.
- Being conversational AI, not rule-based, it adapts to answers in real time.
- Another AI could mix local social trends with sales history to forecast hits or propose recipes.

### AI/ML on AWS

- With Amazon SageMaker AI you create, train and deploy custom models, while AI services offer pre-built ones (translation, image recognition).
- On demand, they spare businesses of any size the time and cost of building from scratch.
- Training data (sales, social posts, sensors, feedback forms) must be gathered, cleaned and formatted first.

## Key takeaways

- Training builds a model from history, and inference applies it to new data.
- A fixed pairing rule is classical programming, while per-customer suggestions come from ML.
- ML is only as accurate as its data.

## Scenarios

- Pastry suggestions from each customer's history: an ML recommendation engine, a headline ML use case.
- A kiosk taking spoken orders: conversational AI with NLP.

## Terms

- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: Computer systems performing humanlike tasks.
- **Machine learning (ML)**: AI that learns from data patterns, not written instructions.
- **Classical programming**: Hand-coding fixed rules instead of learning them.
- **Training**: Finding patterns in historical data to build a model.
- **Inference**: Applying a trained model to new data.
- **Natural language processing (NLP)**: What lets the course's kiosk grasp speech and ask follow-ups.
- **Conversational AI**: AI that adapts to replies, unlike rule-based systems.
